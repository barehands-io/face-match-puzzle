# GBAM Style Bible v1

## 1. Shape language
Everything begins with soft geometric primitives:
- head: rounded square / soft oval
- cheeks: circles or soft ellipses
- eyes: slightly flattened spheres
- ears: simple rounded C-shapes
- nose: rounded wedge / bean-like form
- mouth: wide arc with simple interior
- hair: large graphic masses first, detail second
- hands/feet: simplified, readable, not anatomical studies

Avoid thin spikes, razor edges, realistic wrinkles, or tiny surface noise.

## 2. Signature face
The face must remain readable at phone-screen size:
- eyes are oversized
- eye spacing is generous
- brows are thick and simple
- cheek accents are visible
- nose is graphic and slightly exaggerated
- mouth is broad and friendly
- ears can be slightly oversized

## 3. Asymmetry
Add 1–3% asymmetry:
- one brow slightly higher
- mouth corner offset by a few millimetres
- braid lengths not perfectly identical
- cheek patches may differ subtly in angle/size
This keeps the characters handmade without looking broken.

## 4. Surface style
Use matte or satin finishes.
No glossy plastic skin.
No photorealistic pores.
Optional low-strength paper/clay grain can be placed in roughness or base colour.

## 5. African-led diversity
The cast can include:
- many brown/deep-brown skin tones
- cornrows
- box braids
- twists
- locs
- afro/coils
- puff buns
- beads
- fades
- headwraps
- kufi
- hijabs
and modern everyday clothing.
Do not make ethnicity depend on one feature alone.

## 6. Hijab construction
Hijab should be a separate mesh.
It wraps around the skull and neck with large clean folds.
Avoid cloth-simulation micro-folds.
Keep 3–6 major fold lines.
Allow colour/pattern swaps through materials.

## 7. Hair construction
Model the hairstyle as readable masses.
Braids are stylised tubes/plaits, not individual hair strands.
The scalp parting pattern can be slightly exaggerated for clarity.

## 8. Emotion
Default emotion = open, cheerful, curious.
Expressions should remain readable and family-friendly.
